Features

What's inside

Everything a paid app splits across subscription tiers — built in, from day one, for free. Five feature sets, and every line below is something the app actually does today, not a roadmap.

Training

  • Build your own plan, import someone else's, or start from one of eight built-in templates — by weekday or rotation, your call.
  • Guided, set by set: a live session timer, RPE tracking, and drag-and-drop reordering — mid-session, not just at setup.
  • A rest timer that trusts real clock time, not a countdown — accurate no matter how long the screen was off.
  • Finish a session and GymTracker tells you what to lift next time — a progressive-overload suggestion, every single time.
  • A full training calendar, complete workout history, and a progress-photo gallery, so the whole story lives in one place.

Strength

  • Bench Press, Squat, and Deadlift: your PR, your history, a trend chart, and a plateau flagged the moment it happens.
  • A strength level in plain English for every lift — Beginner to Elite — plus your Total and DOTS score, no spreadsheet required.
  • A 1RM calculator (Epley formula) and a plate calculator built right in, so math never gets between you and the bar.

Analytics

  • A tab of its own, five sections deep: Overview, Strength, Volume, Consistency, Achievements.
  • Every single number is pulled from sets you actually logged — and GymTracker says so plainly when there isn't enough data yet, instead of faking a chart.
  • Four multi-level achievement paths, calculated live from your real training — nothing pre-scripted, nothing handed to you for free.

Exercise database

  • 535 exercises, searchable and sorted by category, loaded live from GitHub with an offline fallback so you're never stuck.
  • An illustrated, tappable muscle-activation diagram on every single one — front, back, male, female.
  • Missing your exercise? Build it with the same editor the other 535 were built with — tap the muscles directly on the figure.

More

  • Apple Health and Health Connect integration, so your training data talks to the rest of your phone.
  • A daily training reminder that nags exactly once, exactly when you asked it to.
  • Backup and restore through a text export or your OS's native share sheet — no account, no auto-sync, no server holding your data hostage.
  • Custom accent colors, automatic light/dark mode, and 10 languages — it should look and read like it's yours.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Is GymTracker really free?

Yes — no subscription, no in-app purchases, no premium tier hiding behind a paywall. It's open source too: free means free.

Where does my data go?

Nowhere. Everything is stored only on your device. There's no server and no account behind GymTracker — the only network calls are loading the exercise database (with an offline fallback) and checking GitHub for new releases, and you can turn both off.

Does it work offline?

Yes. The whole app works without a connection, exercise database included, thanks to an offline fallback.

Can I sync my data between my phone and my laptop?

Not automatically — there's no cloud account to sync through, on purpose. Move it yourself with a backup: export it as text, or send it through your OS's native share sheet whenever you choose to.

What platforms does it run on?

Android, iOS, Windows, and macOS — four native builds from one codebase, each built automatically by the same release pipeline.

Does GymTracker connect to Apple Health or Health Connect?

Yes, both are supported, so your training data can show up alongside the rest of your health data.

I don't see my exercise in the database — now what?

Build it yourself with the same editor the other 535 exercises were built with — tap the muscles directly on the figure and it's done.
